AI makes it easier than ever to build the wrong thing quickly. Zooming out to see the whole system is one way teams notice. In this episode of Patterns, Chris Strahl talks with James McElroy, a designer and design leader who started in mechanical engineering, about keeping the big picture in view while the tools get faster.  
  
James traces a systems mindset from childhood terminals at home to a first design job at Bose, where a handheld home theater remote with a 128-segment display forced James to be frugal with every segment. From there the conversation moves through Monster.com in the dot-com era, architects at Autodesk, the pendulum problem of AI hype, and how to help colleagues at companies that are not tech native understand what AI can and can't do.  
  
We'll explore:

-   Why the ability to zoom out and see every screen, flow, or piece of hardware is still one of the most compelling things a designer can show a team
-   What designing for a 128-segment display taught James about frugal, systems-level design
-   Why happy paths are largely a myth in complex products, and what the miserable path shows
-   How to help people at companies that are not tech native understand AI, starting with using it daily until its limits show
-   Why agreeing on goals matters more than deciding who owns the screen when everyone can make a mockup
