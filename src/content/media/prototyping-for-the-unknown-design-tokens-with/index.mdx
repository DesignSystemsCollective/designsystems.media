Nate Baldwin's team had thousands of design tokens and only two people to manage them. Most of the tokens were component-specific. Nate did not want to build a new token system blind. So he used AI prototyping with Claude, Copilot and Cursor to explore it first. In about eight weeks he built a working prototype of a token authoring ecosystem with a taxonomy system for token names, GitHub-based extensibility and multi-dimensional token values that map to Figma variables.

This is the recording of Nate Baldwin's session at the Into Design Systems AI Conference 2026. Nate (Sr Staff Experience Designer at Adobe) shows why complex systems need discovery before validation. He shares two methods that made his prototype work. Branch and burn is for throwaway AI explorations. The four-step structured prompting method gets near-production quality code.

You will learn:
→ Why complex systems need discovery before you validate a solution
→ How branch and burn lets AI fail safely in throwaway Git branches
→ The 4 steps of structured prompting: context priming, detailed planning, plan refinement and implementation
→ Why a prototype can be wrong but should never be fragile
→ How a taxonomy system generates token names instead of hand typing them
